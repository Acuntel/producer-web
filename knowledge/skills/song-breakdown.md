# SKILL: breaking a record down and finding its best pieces

You break a song down the way a producer does in a listening session: structure first, then energy, then the one thing that makes it that record. You reason from MEASURED SOUND (the ears), from STEMS when the studio has separated them, and from the DNA of the lane. You never copy a recording; you learn its DNA and write your own notes.

## The breakdown, in order (say it in this order, one breath each)
1. Frame: tempo, key or its relative, lane, and how long until the first drop (the collection median is about 30 seconds, 15 percent into the record).
2. Structure map: the sections with their energy (per-bar energy 0-1 in the facts), where the kick leaves, where the drop lands, how long each block is. Name the shape (intro 16, build 8, drop 16, break 16, drop 32 is a common club shape).
3. The engine: which element carries the identity. In tech-house it is almost always the bass hook; in hard-house the warp bass plus the stab; in melodic house the chord progression; in garage the swung drums and the chop. Say which and why (band shares, what repeats most).
4. The hook: the phrase that repeats at bar 1 and bar 5 of the drop. Note its register, length (one or two bars), rhythm (offbeat or downbeat), and whether it answers the bass.
5. The drum language: kicks per bar, where the clap sits, open-hat placement, ghost notes, swing (facts.groove.swing and the groove map), fills before transitions.
6. The mix picture: sub+low share, mids, air, pump, loudness, width. Compare to the lane DNA.
7. The trick: the one production move that makes the record memorable (a filter that opens for 16 bars, a vocal chop pitched to the fifth, a silence before the drop, a half-time bar). Name it and how to do it here.

## Finding the best pieces (what to take, never the recording itself)
- BEST 8 BARS: the window with the highest mean energy that also contains the most repeated melodic motif; usually bars 1-8 of the first drop. Say the time as m:ss.
- THE DRUM SOUND: from the drums stem, the kick with the cleanest low fundamental (40-60 Hz), the hat with the most consistent transient, the clap with the widest stereo image. The studio can slice these as one-shots; you pitch the kick so its fundamental sits on the root or fifth of the new key.
- THE GROOVE MAP: the measured 16-slot timing of the hats (facts.groove.grooveMap). Borrow it with {"op":"groove","track":"all","name":"custom","from":"reference"}.
- THE BASS IDEA: the rhythm pattern of the bass (which offbeats), not its notes. Write your own notes in the new key.
- THE ARC: section lengths, energy contour, drop position. This is DNA and it is free to emulate.

## Turning a breakdown into moves
- Reference loaded: close the biggest delta first (tempo, then key, then sub+low, then pump, then structure). Say which delta you closed.
- Emulating a named artist: use ARTIST DNA medians for tempo, key family, loudness and band shares; write the hook in that artist's rhythm language, never their melody.
- "Make it like this song" is a rollout (action emulate) unless one element is named; then it is ops.
- Never claim a part is "the same as" the record. Say "in the spirit of" and name the DNA you borrowed.
