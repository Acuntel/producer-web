# SKILL: vocals in a dance record

## What a vocal does in these lanes
- Tech-house: one spoken or sung phrase, dry and close, on the downbeat of the drop and again at bar 5; often a single word chopped on the offbeat.
- Hard-house and speed garage: pitched diva chops, big reverb tail in the breakdown, dry stabs of the same syllable in the drop; the chop sits a fifth or an octave above the sung note.
- Melodic house and trance: a full sung hook in the breakdown, thinned to one line in the drop so the lead can breathe.
- Afro house: call and response, layered low harmonies, percussive syllables.

## How the studio does it today
- Library vocals (SAMPLES AVAILABLE) as one-shots: midi 60 as recorded, 67 a fifth up, 72 an octave up. Tune toward the key with clip.semitones within plus or minus 4 so the formant stays human.
- An uploaded sung phrase: warp to tempo, highpass 150-200 Hz, an 8n delay at wet 0.2-0.3, reverb send 0.2-0.35 in breakdowns and 0.1 in drops.
- Hum to melody: action recordHum for the topline; then a vocal-like lead (pluck or lead with long release) carries it until a singer or a vocal render replaces it.
- Placement law: the vocal owns bar 1 of the drop; the bass owns bars 2-4. Vocal and lead never speak at the same time.

## Writing a topline that lands
- One idea, six to ten syllables, vowels open (ah, oh, ay), repeated at bar 1 and bar 5, varied once at bar 13.
- Rhythm first: write the syllables on the grid as notes, then the pitch. Offbeat entries in garage lanes, downbeat entries in tech-house.
- Range: a fifth to an octave; the hook note is the root or the fifth; the color note is the b3 or b7.
- A vocal render layer (full sung vocals from a model) is a future studio feature. Until it lands, say so and use the tools above.
