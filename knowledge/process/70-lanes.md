# 70 · Lanes: per-artist DNA and reference tracks

BPM and key values are from Tunebat/SongBPM-style databases [S60] unless noted; treat keys as analyzer estimates (Losing It is listed as D major by Tunebat and G major by the deconstruction site [S59]; the bass sits on G/G#).

## Tech house (124-130)

### John Summit
- Tempo 126-127. Keys: A minor (Where You Are), E minor (Shiver), C# minor (Deep End) [S60].
- Process: 8-bar chord loop or Splice sample first, MIDI drawn by mouse, bass and groove next, arrangement last; 30 minutes per sketch, months of producing out on the road; laptop speakers then headphones; ear candy last [S1]. Diva for the thick analogue basslines, Nexus strings, Sylenth1, Decapitator on vocals [S1].
- Signature: big vocal topline over a tech-house groove; the string/pad lift into the drop; bridges tech house, house and techno [S90].
- Habits: 25 studio sessions in 30 days for the album foundations [S1].
- References: "Deep End" (2020) for the bass-as-hook tech-house template; "Where You Are" (2023, with HAYLA) for the vocal-led festival build; "Shiver" (2024, with HAYLA) for the euphoric pad + pluck layering; "Everything Always" (with Dom Dolla) for the collaboration blueprint [S90].

### Dom Dolla
- Tempo 125-130. Take It 125 C minor; Rhyme Dust 128 C minor (with MK); Saving Up 130 E♭ minor [S60].
- Process: entirely in the box on a MacBook, written on couches and hotel beds; rotates four headphones plus earbuds and laptop speakers; Serum/Massive basses split into a sub channel and a top-grit channel; validates tracks in club sets before labels [S3]. Builds around old vocal loops chopped and pitched into new hooks; arranges for the dancefloor: when to tease the vocal, when to drop the drums [S3].
- References: "Take It" (2018) for the one-line vocal hook plus rolling bass; "San Frandisco" (2019) for the disco-sample tech house; "Rhyme Dust" (2023) for the rap-vocal chop drop; "Saving Up" (2024) for the faster (130) melodic pop-tech-house crossover.

### Chris Lake
- Tempo 125 (Operator, I Want You, Turn Off the Lights) [S60]; range quoted 124-130 [S90]. Keys: A♭ major, G♭ minor, B minor [S60]; tutorials use G or A minor [S21].
- Process: laptop only, QWERTY keyboard, built-in speakers; 80% of the music made anywhere; "I Remember" released almost unchanged from the first laptop mix [S2]. Minimal, heavily processed: kick + plucky bass are the record, everything else orbits [S20]. Serum 2 wavetable bass -2 octaves, 1.2 s decay, velocity-to-cutoff, OTT 15%, full-ratio sidechain; snare reverb 0.4 s at 15% [S20].
- Signature: bouncy offbeat bass, cutoff automation as the main movement tool, vocal hooks with attitude; co-writer on Fisher records; "925" with Sammy Virji [S5][S20].
- References: "Operator (Ring Ring)" (2016) for the vocal-chop hook; "I Want You" (2016) for the pluck bass; "Turn Off the Lights" (2019, Alexis Roberts) for the vocal-first arrangement; Black Book Records catalogue for the current sound.

### FISHER
- Tempo 125-127. Losing It 125; Just Feels Tight 127 C#/D♭; Take It Off 127 C#/D♭ [S60].
- Process: one 4-bar bass loop on two notes (G/G#) with four variants; lead is a 4-bar phrase driven by automation; drums escalate per drop (rimshots in drop 2, rides in drop 3, kick to 8ths in build 2 and 16ths at the end); Sylenth1 plus stock Ableton devices; breakdown→build→drop three times then a different final build; 6:40 club mix opening with 8 bars of filtered kicks [S59]. Mixed and mastered by Kevin Grainger and Tom Norris; written with Chris Lake's support [S91].
- Signature: louder, bigger, built for crowds; mid-range aggressive basslines; one shouted hook [S90].
- References: "Losing It" (2018) for the single-riff escalation model; "Just Feels Tight" (2021) and "Take It Off" (2023) for the 127-BPM festival tech-house with pop vocal.

## Hard house / speed garage / UKG (132-145)

### KETTAMA
- Tempo 142-144 for the rave/hard-house cuts: Fly Away XTC 142 G# minor; Yosemite 144 A♭ (with Interplanetary Criminal); Air Maxes 142 (listed as 71 half-time) B♭ minor (with Fred again.. and Shady Nasty) [S60].
- Process: FL Studio treated like an MPC, "creativity comes from limitation", all in the box, loop-based and sample-heavy, "whatever comes out, comes out" [S6][S7]. Drums: distorted 909-style kick with extra bite from Fruity Destructor, four layered loops with a full breakbeat as the first top layer, ~30% distortion mix on the loop group, +10 kHz boost, fills at the end of every 4 and 8 bars, dissonant rave-stab notes (A# against A) [S58].
- Signature: warehouse hard-house energy, hoover/rave stabs, orchestral/trance melodies over G-Town drums, half-time drops; influences Head High (Shed), Thomas Bangalter, Grant Nelson's piano [S6][S7].
- References: "B O D Y" (2018) for the raw house start; "G-Town 001" EP (2021) for the warehouse template; "Fly Away XTC" (2022) for the 142 hard-house/trance hybrid; "Yosemite" (2025) for the KETTAMA × IC speed-garage crossover; "Archangel" LP (Oct 2025) for the current album-scale arrangements [S7].

### Interplanetary Criminal
- Tempo 128-138: Supreme Level 130 C#; B.O.T.A. 137 C (with Eliza Rose, UK #1 2022); Damager 138 B minor (with Sammy Virji); Yosemite 144 [S60].
- Process (Attack's Supreme Level breakdown): 128 BPM, kick and rim fills on a 16th-triplet grid with velocity variation, kick enters bar 3, church bell on the first downbeat, hats on every offbeat sent -25 dB to a 1/8 echo at 40% feedback, two chord samples with the second +5 st, bass hits high-passed at 100 Hz plus sub samples (D#2, C#2, D#1), compressor sidechain with attack at minimum and 4-5 dB reduction, Amen slices at -1/-4 st, vinyl crackle, glue 2-3 dB with the slowest attack [S18].
- Signature: organ-y speed garage, 2-step skip, Manchester/Bolton heritage; influences El-B, Jeremy Sylvester, Zed Bias [S8].
- References: "Supreme Level" (2021) for the bass-garage-2-step pattern; "B.O.T.A." (2022) for the organ hook and vocal; "Damager" (2024) for the 138 speed-garage collaboration sound; "All Thru The Night" compilation (2023) for the label palette [S8].

### Sammy Virji
- Tempo 132-138: If U Need It 132 B♭ minor; Peach 135 C#/D♭ (with salute); Damager 138 B minor [S60].
- Process: started at 12 on GarageBand/Logic; "if it sounds good, it's good"; mixes in key obsessively; turns master tempo off on drops for speaker quality; elongated notes in F# because "F-sharp is the best note to rattle a sub"; FM bassline on "Never Let You Go" (a Double 99 "Ripgroove" flip with elongated vocals and changed bass notes) [S4][S70].
- Signature: 4/4 bassline foundations with Nice 'N' Ripe sharpness, crunchy Night Bass drums, "swingy chugging drums", jazz chords, R&B key changes, vibrato 2-step vocal sampling, horns (father Fayyaz on trombone), dubstep-grade sound design kept as a weapon [S4][S5].
- References: "Never Let You Go" (2017) for the FM bass + Ripgroove vocal; "5 Star" (2022) for horns over garage; "If U Need It" (2023) for the 132 vocal garage hit; "Peach" (2024) for key-change euphoria with salute; "Damager" (2024) for speed garage at 138; "925" (with Chris Lake and RoRo) for the tech-house crossover [S4][S5].

### salute
- Tempo 132-135: Joy 132 E major; Wait For It 133 B♭ minor; Peach 135; system 104 (non-garage) [S60].
- Process: started at 13 on Fruity Loops; wrote ~30 ideas in a week with Karma Kid and Bondax's Adam Kaye, then selected; vintage digital gloss (Roland D-50, Yamaha DX7), pumping Deadmau5-style sidechain; influences Daft Punk, DJ Falcon, Alan Braxe, Todd Edwards 2-step, MJ Cole, Cassius vocal manipulation [S9].
- Signature: euphoric French-house-meets-garage, glassy keys, vocal samples, nostalgic loops [S9].
- References: "Joy" (2021, LEILAH) and "Wait For It" (2023) for the glossy-key garage with emotional vocal; "Ultra Pool" (2022) for the Steel City Dance Discs club side; "Shield" EP (2023) for the album-scale sound [S9].
